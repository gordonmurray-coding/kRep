Anyone can claim a good track record. This checks it.

You are about to send money to a stranger who says they are reliable. Paste the record they gave you and find out how much of it is actually provable.

Why this beats asking around

Reviews
Free to write, so free to fake. Fifty glowing accounts cost nothing but an afternoon.
Platform ratings
Harder to fake — but the platform owns them. Leave and you start at zero, and you are trusting the platform’s word about its own sellers.
A record like this
Every deal in it had to be paid for with a real transaction on Kaspa’s public chain, and the failures are written by the escrow itself rather than by the person being judged. Faking a history means buying it, deal by deal, over the months it claims to span — and the record belongs to them, not to any platform.
Safe to paste: a record holds no passwords, no keys, and no way to move anyone’s money.

What this can and can’t tell you

The record isn’t stored here, or anywhere they control. Every deal on the list was written into a Kaspa transaction — a public payment that really settled, at a moment nobody can move. This page looks each one up on a node. If a deal isn’t there it doesn’t count, however convincing the file looks.

They can’t quietly drop the bad ones. Entries are chained, each sealed over the one before, so removing an embarrassing deal from the middle breaks the file and this page says so. And when someone takes a job and vanishes, the escrow writes that failure itself — no signature from them required, because a defaulter would never sign one.

It cannot tell you who they are. These are nicknames. That is deliberate: a record you can carry between marketplaces without carrying your name with it.

It cannot tell you everything they have done. A record can stop early. Removing an entry from the middle breaks the chain, but ending it before the embarrassing part does not — so read a clean record as the least they have done, not all of it.

It cannot tell you the deals were at arm’s length. Two friends trading back and forth build a real, paid-for record that recommends nobody. Watch how many different people appear below; the page flags it when they are few.
