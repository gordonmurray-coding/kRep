How can a file like this be trusted?

The record isn’t stored here, or anywhere they control. Every deal on the list was written into a Kaspa transaction — a public payment that really settled, at a moment in time nobody can move. This page goes to a Kaspa node and looks each one up. If a deal isn’t there, it doesn’t count, no matter how convincing the file looks.

Lying is possible, but it isn’t free. Anyone can invent a glowing history in a text editor. What they can’t do is invent the transactions behind it: each entry cost real fees to publish, and the deals themselves ran through an escrow that held real money. A fake history has to be paid for, deal by deal, over the months it claims to span.

They can’t quietly drop the bad ones. The entries are chained together, each one sealed over the one before, so removing an embarrassing deal breaks the file and this page says so. And when someone takes a job and vanishes, the escrow itself writes the failure to the chain — no signature from them required.

What it still can’t tell you. Not who they are: these are nicknames, not names. Not everything they’ve done: they can show you a short record instead of a long one, and you’d never know. And not whether the deals were real — two friends can trade with each other all day to build a record. Watch how many different people appear below.
